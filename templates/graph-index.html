The pilot studies as discourse graphs

Each path in the pilot corpus drawn in the grammar of Discourse Graphs: the question, the hypothesis, the experiments that addressed it, the results they produced, and the edge each result earns against the hypothesis. A negative is drawn as opposing the hypothesis only when the informativeness rule counts it as evidence of absence; otherwise it is merely consistent with it.

How the two models line up

Discourse Graphs is a grammar for arguing: typed one-sentence notes, linked by whether they support or oppose one another. The Negative-Results Record is a format for recording an attempt: what was tried, under what conditions, with which controls, and whether the negative counts. The graphs on this page are the first drawn over the second. Every node and edge comes from a field the record already holds, and nothing was added to the schema to draw them.

The last row is the interesting one. The Discourse Graphs lab conventions say a claim is supported by a result only if certain conditions hold, and otherwise the result is merely consistent with it, and the conditions are left as placeholders for each lab to fill in. The record fills them in with code: a negative supports the absence of an effect only if a positive control passed, the negative control stayed clean, and a sensitivity or power statement exists. That rule is what decides, on every graph here, whether a red edge is drawn or a dashed grey one.

The Discourse Graphs grammar is described at discoursegraphs.com and in Chan and Akamatsu’s 2024 preprint, arXiv:2407.20666. The schema is at portolansoft.com/schema.
