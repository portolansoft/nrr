The graph

Read left to right. The question (QUE) is addressed by {{n_exp}} experiments (EXP), each of which produces results (RES), and each result bears on the hypothesis (HYP) with one of four edges. {{n_supports}} support it, {{n_opposes}} oppose it, {{n_informs}} inform it, and {{n_consistent}} are merely consistent with it because the informativeness rule would not count them as evidence either way. Click a node to jump to its full text below.

{{svg}}
  • supports positive finding
  • opposes informative negative, or refutation
  • informs partial finding
  • consistent with inconclusive or uninformative: no positive control passed, or no sensitivity statement
  • thin grey a relation the record itself states between two attempts
  • dashed box a result the informativeness rule marks uninformative, or an issue

The grammar is that of Discourse Graphs (Chan and Akamatsu): QUE, HYP, EXP, RES and ISS nodes with supports, opposes and informs edges. Every node and edge is derived from fields already in the Negative-Results Record; the edge a finding earns is decided by its outcomeClass and informativeness. Issues (ISS) hang below the graph as a strip: the open questions and untried branches the path leaves for someone else to pick up.
